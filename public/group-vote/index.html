    A poll without the palaver

    Most "quick votes" die in the group chat: forty replies, three people voting for options that scrolled off the top, and no clear answer. Group Vote is the boring fix. You write the question and the options, you get one link, everyone taps it and picks. The running tally is right there, so the decision makes itself.

    What it's good for

    Anything a group has to agree on and nobody wants to chair a meeting about. A book club's next read. A baby name shortlist. Where to eat on Friday. Which date works for the dinner — tick "pick more than one" and it becomes a when-are-you-free poll. Team name, tip jar cause, the office coffee order: if it's a shared call, this sorts it.

    How it works

    1. Write the question and options

    One option per line, two to thirty of them. Decide whether people pick just one or as many as they like, and whether voters can add their own option when you've missed something obvious.

    2. Share the link

    You get two links: a share link for the group chat, and an organiser link that shows who voted, lets you add or remove options, and closes the poll when you're done. Bookmark the organiser one — there are no accounts, so the link is the only key.

    3. Watch the bars fill in

    Each vote nudges the bars; the leader sits in gold. Voters see the results the moment they've voted, and can change their mind any time until you close it. When you close voting, the tally freezes and the winner's called.

    FAQ

    Is it actually free?

    Yes. No account, no trial, no card. It's a small tool, not a platform.

    Is it really one vote per person?

    On the honour system. There are no accounts, so a browser remembers a person's vote — but a determined voter on two phones could vote twice. It's built for friendly group decisions, not elections. If you need a locked-down ballot, this isn't it, and it says so honestly.

    Can voters add their own options?

    If you switch it on, yes — a voter can add an option that isn't listed and vote for it in one go. Leave it off and the list stays exactly as you set it.

    Can I change my vote?

    Yes, any time until the poll's closed. Your browser remembers your vote, so "change my vote" is right there on the results.

    Who can see how I voted?

    Only the organiser sees the breakdown of who picked what. Everyone else — anyone with the share link — sees just the totals.
