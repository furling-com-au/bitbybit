    What it's for

    Most people know about a third of the organisation and then stop. Not because anyone is unfriendly — there is simply no reason to talk to someone two teams over, and no natural moment to start. Coffee roulette manufactures the moment: you get paired with someone, there is a reason to say hello, and twenty minutes later you know one more person.

    It matters more in a hybrid team, where the corridor conversation that used to do this job stopped happening and nothing replaced it.

    How it works

    1. Paste the names, share one link

    The names go in, everyone gets the same shared link. There is no invite list, no email addresses, and nothing to install. If you can put a link in a group chat, you can run this.

    2. Everyone claims their name once

    Each person taps their own name and lands on a private page. That page is theirs — bookmark it. Nobody else can see it, including you.

    3. Draw the next round whenever you like

    One button re-pairs everybody. Nobody has to claim their name again — the link each person already has simply starts showing their new partner. That is the whole difference between a tool people use twice and one they use all year.

    The details that matter

    • Odd numbers make a three, not a leftover. If your team has eleven people, one group is a trio. Nobody sits out, and nobody has to be told they were the odd one.
    • Recent pairings are avoided. The draw remembers the last few rounds and tries not to repeat a pairing. It is a soft rule — on a small team a repeat eventually has to happen, and a round that happens beats a round that is perfect.
    • Every pair gets a conversation starter. An actual question, not "get to know each other". It is the first two minutes that are awkward, and a question skips them.
    • The organiser can't see the pairings. You can see who has claimed a name. Who got who lives only on each person's own page.

    Why not a Slack or Teams app?

    Because most people can't install one. Donut, CoffeePals and the rest are workspace apps, and adding a workspace app means an admin, a ticket and sometimes a procurement conversation. A link is not procurement. If you can paste a URL into a channel, you can start this afternoon without asking anybody.

    The trade is honest: an app can nudge people in Slack and this cannot. This sends no notifications at all. You post the link when a new round is drawn, the same way you would post anything else.

    FAQ

    Do people need an account?

    No. Nobody signs up, nobody gives an email address, and there is nothing to install. Each person's page is a long unguessable link — holding the link is the whole thing.

    What happens with an odd number of people?

    One group of three. Nobody is left out and nobody has to be told they drew the short straw.

    Does everyone have to re-claim their name each round?

    No, and this is the point. Claiming happens once. After that each person's private link keeps working, and every new round it shows a new partner.

    Can I see who got paired with who?

    No — not even as the organiser. The pairing only appears on each person's own private page. You can see who has claimed a name, so you know who still needs a nudge.

    What if someone loses their link?

    Reset them from your organiser page. That rotates their private link and lets them claim their name again. Their old link stops working.

    Can I use it for something other than coffee?

    Yes. It is a pairing draw with a conversation starter attached — people use the same thing for walking meetings, lunch pairs, mentoring rounds and onboarding buddies. The tool does not care what you do when you get there.
