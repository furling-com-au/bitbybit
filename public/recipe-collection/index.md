## A recipe book, made by everyone

Some of the best cookbooks aren't bought — they're compiled. A **collaborative recipe book** is the gift where everyone puts in one recipe and the person on the receiving end gets a whole kitchen's worth of them, in the handwriting of the people who taught them. This is the tool that gathers them without a group email thread forty replies deep.

It suits a bridal or kitchen-tea shower, a farewell for someone moving on, a milestone birthday, a new home, or a family reunion where the recipes have only ever lived in someone's head. Start a book, share one link, and each person adds their recipe — the ingredients, the method, and the little story that goes with it.

## How it works

### 1. Start the book

Name it — *Grandma Rosa's Recipe Book*, *The Patel Family Collection* — and say who it's for if you like. You get two links: a **share link** for everyone contributing, and an **organiser link** that can tidy up and print. Bookmark the organiser one; there are no accounts, so the link is the only key.

### 2. Everyone adds a recipe

Contributors tap the link and fill in their recipe: who's sharing it, the dish, the ingredients, the method, and optionally a memory of why it matters. No sign-up. Their browser remembers which recipes are theirs, so they can fix a typo or add the step they forgot. Two people can share a recipe under the same name — two Mums, two Sams, no problem.

### 3. Print the keepsake

Every recipe lands in one nicely set-out book. When you're ready, hit **Print the book** — the print view drops all the buttons and forms and keeps each recipe on its own, so it comes out looking like a proper little cookbook you can bind, frame, or slip into a card.

## FAQ

### Is the recipe book actually free?

Yes. No account, no trial, no card. It's a small tool made for the joy of it, not a platform. A **recipe book gift, free** and without the catch.

### Do contributors need an account?

No — they tap the link, write their recipe, done. Their browser remembers their own recipes so they can edit or remove them; nobody signs up for anything.

### Can I use it for a family recipe collection?

Absolutely — that's a favourite. A **family recipe collection online** means the recipes that only ever lived in someone's head finally get written down, in everyone's own words, before they're lost.

### Can two people add a recipe with the same name?

Yes. Duplicate contributor names are fine — the book happily holds three recipes all "shared by Mum".

### Does it print well?

That's the whole point. Each recipe is set to stay together on the page, the on-screen chrome disappears, and you're left with a clean book to print at home or take to a copy shop. Ideal for a **shower recipe collection** handed over on the day.

### Does it handle money?

No — deliberately. It gathers recipes; there's nothing to pay and nothing to collect. Just a book, made together.
