Free · no signup · one shareable link

The office Cup sweep without the scissors, the hat, or the soggy bits of paper. Paste everyone's names, hit draw, and share the link — 24 horses dealt out fairly before the race that stops the nation.

Not sure what you get? [See a finished sweep →](https://bitibybit.com/s/demo-melbourne-cup-sweep)

## How it works

### 1. Set it up

The horses box comes prefilled with Horse 1 through Horse 24, because the real field isn't confirmed until the Saturday before the race. Two ways to run it: draw now with numbers and match them to the printed field on the day — every paper and the VRC site carry it — or hold off until the declarations and paste the actual names in. Short field this year? Delete a few lines.

### 2. Draw

Paste everyone's names and hit draw. The shuffle uses your browser's cryptographic randomness — nobody can rig it, including us. Fewer people than horses is the normal state of a Cup sweep: names cycle fairly, so everyone draws the same number of horses, give or take one. Ten people, twenty-four horses, plenty to yell about at 3pm.

### 3. Share the link

You get two links: a **share link** that shows everyone the drawn board, and an **organiser link** that lets you re-draw or delete. Bookmark the organiser one — there are no accounts, so the link is the only key. The shared page prints cleanly for the tea-room fridge.

Want the conventions in full — pot splits, scratchings, entry etiquette? See the [Cup sweep rules](https://bitibybit.com/melbourne-cup-sweep/rules/), or grab the [printable sheet](https://bitibybit.com/melbourne-cup-sweep/printable/) if your office insists on scissors.

## FAQ

### Is it actually free?

Yes. No account, no trial, no card. It's a small tool, not a platform.

### How many people do we need?

Anywhere from two to twenty-four. Fewer people than horses is how a Cup sweep is meant to work — everyone just draws more than one. More than 24 and someone misses out, so run a second sweep for the overflow.

### What about scratchings?

Agree the house rule before the race, while everyone's still friends. The usual options: a scratched horse's holder gets their money back, scratchings share last place's prize, or the whole board gets re-drawn once the final field is locked in. If you go the re-draw route, the organiser link has a button for it — one click, no whiteboard.

### Does it handle money?

No — deliberately. Collect the gold coins however your office always has. This just does the drawing and the remembering.

### Can I print it?

The drawn board prints cleanly on one page — open the shared link and hit print. If you were searching for a printable sweep template to cut into 24 bits of paper: this is the same thing, minus the scissors.

### When is the Melbourne Cup?

The first Tuesday in November — 3 November 2026 — jumping at 3pm AEDT. The final field of 24 is declared the Saturday before.
