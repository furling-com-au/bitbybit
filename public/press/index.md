# Press kit

For journalists & newsletters

bitibybit.com is a shelf of free, no-signup tools for the small rituals Australian groups run every year — the office Cup sweep, the Kris Kringle, the street Christmas potluck — drawn in cosy pixel art and deliberately incapable of touching anyone's money.

## The short version

One person makes a thing — a sweep, a name-draw, a potluck board, a gift registry — and shares a single link. No accounts, no app, free to use, no ads, no cookies. It's built and run in Australia as a side project, and the code is open source.

## Story angles

- **The Cup sweep that refuses to touch the money.** Australians run hundreds of thousands of office sweeps on the first Tuesday of November, mostly with scissors and a biscuit tin. This one draws the board in a minute and deliberately handles zero dollars — the gold coins stay exactly where they've always been.
- **The wedding registry drawn as the gift.** Couples saving for something big show it as a pixel drawing; guests claim a part — a tyre, the snorkel, the $15 air freshener — and the picture paints itself in as it's funded. No platform fees; money goes guest-to-couple by PayID.
- **Kris Kringle without the emails.** The incumbent name-draw tools all want everyone's email address. This one works in the group chat: share a link, claim your name, see only your person.

## Images

Free to use in coverage. Right-click, save.

Every image on the site is generated from declarative shape tables by a couple of hundred lines of code — no image editor involved — which is its own small story for the technically inclined.

## Facts

- Free, no accounts, no ads, no cookies, no money handled
- 22 tools live; more each season
- Made in Australia; open source on [GitHub](https://github.com/furling-com-au/bitbybit)
- Built on Cloudflare's edge network — pages load fast anywhere
