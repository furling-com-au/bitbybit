## The canteen is not run by the school

In most Australian government schools the canteen belongs to the parent body, not the school — the P&C in state schools, the P&F in most Catholic and independent ones, and in Victoria the school council, an elected board of parents, staff and community members. The school provides the premises; the body operating the canteen is the employer, carries the legal responsibility and keeps the profit. That decides who builds the roster and who answers for it when nobody turns up on Thursday.

The staffing model is close to universal: one paid person plus rostered volunteers. That person is the tuckshop convenor in Queensland, canteen manager elsewhere, employed by the parent body rather than the department. They do menu, ordering, food safety, cash and supervision; volunteers do prep, bagging and counter under their direction.

## Do not make the convenor build the roster

Rostering defaults to the convenor, so the person cooking is also the person texting at 8pm to fill Friday. Schools that run well split it off: a named volunteer roster coordinator who is a parent, with their own email address, so canteen questions and roster questions stop landing in one inbox.

## A canteen day, hour by hour

A primary canteen day in the shape schools publish it.

| Time | What happens | Shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9.10am | Arrive, sign in at the office | Morning |
| 9.15am | Open up, print and collate online orders by class, warmer on | Morning |
| 10.00am | Morning tea food, start lunch prep | Morning |
| 11.20am | Serve recess | Morning |
| 11.40am | Wash up, hand over | Both — overlap |
| 11.50am | Load warmer, prepare lunch | Afternoon |
| 12.40pm | Bag hot items, cutlery in, pack class baskets | Afternoon |
| 1.10pm | Classes collect baskets | Afternoon |
| 1.20pm | Serve at the counter | Afternoon |
| 1.50pm | Close up, clean down | Afternoon |
| 2.00pm | Sign out | Afternoon |

Offer three options: **all day 9.10am–2.00pm**, **morning 9.10–11.50am**, **afternoon 11.40am–2.00pm**. All day is cleaner and worth asking for first, because a split costs a handover mid-prep. Offer the split anyway, because it is what fills — and keep the ten minutes of overlap at 11.40, the only reason the afternoon volunteer knows what is in the warmer.

Two other shapes are worth stealing. Some primaries add a **bagging shift, 12.30–2.30pm**: short, low-skill, high-volume, and the easiest slot in the term to fill for a nervous first-timer. Secondary canteens run roughly 8.30am to 2.15pm on three to four volunteers; their break times often differ by day, so write the real recess and lunch time on each dated row, not in the header.

## Count the slots before you promise anything

An Australian term runs nine to eleven weeks. A three-day canteen with two shifts a day is about 60 filled slots; a five-day secondary canteen needing four volunteers is closer to 200. Put that total at the top of the sheet.

- Small primary, one to three days: convenor plus two volunteers a day.
- Medium primary, five days: convenor plus two to three a day.
- Secondary, five days: one or two paid managers plus three to four a day.
- In every case a named reserve list. Not "ring around" — names.

Three days a week is normal, and far easier to staff than a five-day roster full of holes.

## Name the jobs, including the one that is not a shift

"Canteen help 9–2" tells a new volunteer nothing. The real jobs: order collation, food prep, bagging and basket packing, counter at recess, counter at lunch, restocking fridges, EFTPOS and student cards, wash-up and clean-down. Online pre-ordering created the collation job and front-loaded the day, which is why the first shift now begins before the bell rather than after it.

Add a **laundry line**. Tea towels and aprons washed at home is a real rostered job, and the one thing to offer a parent who cannot attend at all. Put the perks on the roster too: free lunch on shift, tea and coffee, expenses reimbursed with a receipt.

## The column everyone leaves off

Sign in and sign out. Volunteers sign in at the office or scan a QR code, and that record is what the school and your parent body's volunteer insurer will ask for if someone is injured or a child protection question arises later.

Use the notes column for canteen-specific things. Hair tied back or a net. No rings, bracelets or long nails on the prep bench. Where the anaphylaxis plan and first aid kit live, because a canteen handles allergens all day. And the one that matters most: anyone who has had vomiting or diarrhoea stays home — canteens commonly ask for 48 hours clear, and under the Food Standards Code a food handler with symptoms that may indicate a foodborne illness must not handle food.

## Food rules: who actually decides

Canteen food rules vary by state and territory, and a school canteen is treated differently again from a one-off fundraising stall. Within a state it depends on what you sell, whether it is eaten straight after cooking, and whether you are a non-profit. That combination is why advice from another state — or from a club two suburbs over — is unreliable, and why this page will not tell you what applies to you. Getting it wrong runs both ways: groups buy certification they never needed, and groups skip an obligation they had.

The people who can actually answer it:

- **Your local council's environmental health officer.** They handle notification, permits and inspection, and they will take a phone call from a volunteer committee. Ring before you print a roster, not after.
- **Your state food authority** — the [NSW Food Authority](https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/retail/school-canteens) in NSW, [Queensland Health](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/1156465/non-profit-guideline.pdf) in Queensland, and the equivalent in each other state and territory.
- [Food Standards Australia New Zealand](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/charities) publishes the national Food Standards Code and specific guidance for charities and community groups.
- **Your own parent body or committee.** The P&C, P&F, school council or club committee usually holds the insurance and very often already has a standing answer from last year.

Two things hold everywhere. The food you sell has to be safe and suitable, and the people handling it need skills and knowledge appropriate to what they are doing. Everything past that is local, and worth ten minutes on the phone.

## Common questions

### How do you make a school canteen roster?

Set the shifts first, then find the names. Build a grid of weeks down and shift slots across for the whole term, give every job a real title instead of writing "canteen help", add a sign-in column and a notes column, and put the total slot count at the top. Then hand the roster to a parent coordinator rather than to the paid convenor, who already has a job.

### How many volunteers does a school canteen need?

A small primary open one to three days a week runs on the paid convenor plus two volunteers a day, roughly one per shift. A medium primary open five days needs two to three a day. A secondary canteen needs three to four a day alongside one or two paid managers. Keep a named reserve list in every case, not a vague plan to ring around.

### How long should a canteen volunteer shift be?

An all-day shift of about 9am to 2pm is the cleanest, because splitting the day means handing over in the middle of lunch prep. Most schools offer the split anyway, since it is what fills: a morning of about two and a half hours, an afternoon of about the same, and ten minutes of overlap for the handover. Shorter works too, and a two-hour bagging shift in the middle of the day is usually the easiest slot in the term to fill.

### Do school canteen volunteers need a working with children check?

It depends on the state and on who the volunteer is. Most states exempt a parent volunteering at their own child's school, but the exemption is drawn differently in each jurisdiction, and a grandparent or community volunteer may not be covered by it. Ask the school office and your state screening body rather than relying on advice written for another state.

### Does a school canteen need a food safety supervisor?

Sometimes, and it turns on what the canteen actually does rather than on it being a canteen. The requirement depends on the kind of food handled — unpackaged, ready-to-eat and potentially hazardous food is treated differently from packaged snacks — and it differs by state and by whether the school is government, Catholic or independent. Ring your council's environmental health officer and your state food authority before anyone buys training. It is a short call, and assuming wrongly is expensive in both directions: groups pay for certification they never needed, and groups skip an obligation they had.

### Who runs the school canteen?

In most Australian government schools the parent body runs it, not the school — the parents and citizens association in state schools, parents and friends in most Catholic and independent schools. Victoria differs: the canteen sits under the school council, an elected board of parents, staff and community members. Either way the school provides the premises, and the operating body employs the convenor, carries the legal responsibility and keeps the profit.
