How many people you actually need

The figure most groups quote is sixteen — four shifts of four. It is also two too many at 8am and two short at lunchtime, which is why the template above is weighted to the middle of the day instead.

A realistic full-day minimum is 12 to 14 people plus a coordinator, weighted towards the middle of the day. Trade does not build to a neat noon peak either: a hardware car park fills from about 10.30am, so the shift to overstaff is the one starting at 10.

The seven jobs

  • BBQ Captain (Bunnings' term; some groups say coordinator) — the one role the pack asks you to nominate. On site the whole time, runs the cleans, reports the expense and fundraising totals at the end of the day. Do not also roster them on the tongs.
  • Cook — one to two people, 18 or over. The job people burn out on fastest, so rotate it even within a shift.
  • Bread and onions — onion on the bread first, sausage on top, wrap, pass. Bunnings suggests that order because cooked onion on concrete is a slip hazard. This is the bottleneck at peak, not the grill.
  • Serving — takes the order, calls it to the grill, hands it over.
  • Money — float, tin, and card or QR payments. The pack is explicit that money is handled by people who are not cooking or handling food. If you use Bunnings' mobile payments, the captain registers the group first.
  • Restock runner — keeps raw product covered and below 5°C, and does the shops run if you are heading for a sell-out.
  • Cleaner — the pack asks for the BBQ area to be cleaned at least three times during the day. Put a name and a time against each one, or none of them happen.

Who is allowed to do what

This one breaks scout, cadet and junior sport rosters, where the obvious volunteers are teenagers. Nobody 12 or under has a role at the BBQ, including standing behind it. 13 to 17 can handle money, take orders and serve, but cannot cook or operate equipment. Only 18 and over cook, and gas bottles are connected and changed by a Bunnings team member only. So every shift needs an adult on the grill, however many willing fifteen-year-olds you have.

A car park sizzle is not usually child-related work under the state Working with Children Check schemes, but the schemes differ and your own club or school policy may still require a check.

The sell-out rule that breaks rosters

The pack asks you to be operational through the prime selling period, and that window varies — recent packs state 8am to 3pm as a minimum, older store packs 9am to 4pm. Read yours, because this rule is pinned to that finish time: if you sell out of sausages or bread before it, you are required to buy more and keep serving until it. Hence a named runner with a car, rather than a job the cook absorbs at 11.40am.

The paperwork gate

  • Store packs ask for the signed confirmation form and your council food permit, where one is issued, to reach the Activities Organiser four weeks before the date. One pack is blunt about what follows: if both are not in by three weeks prior, the date is cancelled and issued to another group.
  • You need a current public liability certificate of currency. $10 million is the usual minimum and some stores ask for $20 million, so take the figure from your own pack.
  • Popular metro stores are booked months ahead, sometimes a year. Book first, roster later.
  • Fourteen days' notice to cancel.

Food rules: who actually decides

Rules for a fundraising barbecue vary by state and by council. Within a state it depends on what you sell, whether it is eaten straight after cooking, and whether you are a non-profit. That combination is why advice from another state — or from a club two suburbs over — is unreliable, and why this page will not tell you what applies to you. Getting it wrong runs both ways: groups buy certification they never needed, and groups skip an obligation they had.

The people who can actually answer it:

  • Your local council's environmental health officer. They handle notification, permits and inspection, and they will take a phone call from a volunteer committee. Ring before you print a roster, not after.
  • Your state food authority — the NSW Food Authority in NSW, Queensland Health in Queensland, and the equivalent in each other state and territory.
  • Food Standards Australia New Zealand publishes the national Food Standards Code and specific guidance for charities and community groups.
  • Your own parent body or committee. The P&C, P&F, school council or club committee usually holds the insurance and very often already has a standing answer from last year.

Two things hold everywhere. The food you sell has to be safe and suitable, and the people handling it need skills and knowledge appropriate to what they are doing. Everything past that is local, and worth ten minutes on the phone.

What goes wrong

It is mostly the ends of the day, not the middle. The 2–4 shift evaporates at 3.55 and three people do a pack-down built for six, so name pack-down as its own slot with its own people. It is a real job: the pad has to be degreased, and all rubbish goes home in someone's car rather than into a Bunnings bin.

The rest repeats everywhere: the three cleans have nobody's name against them, so they happen once; drinks get under-ordered; and the captain ends up on the tongs by 10.30, leaving nobody to run handovers or keep the expense total the end-of-day report asks for.

Common questions

How many volunteers do you need for a sausage sizzle?

Twelve to fourteen people plus a coordinator is a workable full day, and sixteen is the figure most groups plan around. Weight them towards the middle: three at 8am, five from 10am, five through 12–2pm, three in the afternoon, plus separate crews for bump-in and pack-down and a restock runner with a car.

What time does a Bunnings sausage sizzle start and finish?

The pack asks you to be operational throughout the prime selling period, and the stated window varies by store and by pack version — recent packs say 8am to 3pm as a minimum, older ones say 9am to 4pm. Check the pack for your store, then add about half an hour of bump-in and an hour of pack-down, which makes it a nine or ten hour day for the coordinator.

Can teenagers help at a Bunnings sausage sizzle?

Not on the grill. Bunnings' pack says nobody aged 12 or under has a role at the BBQ, including standing behind it, and 13 to 17 year olds can handle money, take orders and serve food but cannot cook or operate equipment. Only people 18 and over cook, and gas bottles are connected and changed by a Bunnings team member. Check your own club or school policy on Working with Children Checks for whoever supervises them.

Do you need a food safety certificate to run a sausage sizzle?

Ring your council — that is the honest answer, and it takes five minutes. Fundraising barbecues are frequently treated more lightly than a commercial food stall, but the rules vary by state and by what you sell, and the venue may add requirements of its own on top. Whatever the answer, the food still has to be safe and whoever is on the tongs still needs to know what they are doing.

What happens if you sell out of sausages before the finish time?

You are required to buy more and keep serving until the finish time stated in your pack. That is why the roster needs a named restock runner with a car from mid-morning, rather than sending a cook away from the grill at 11.40am.

How far ahead do you need to book?

Apply to your store's Activities Organiser well in advance — popular metro stores are booked months out and sometimes a year. Once you have a date, store packs ask for the signed confirmation form and your council food permit four weeks before, and at least one pack states that a date is cancelled and issued to another group if they are not in three weeks prior. Cancelling needs fourteen days' notice.
