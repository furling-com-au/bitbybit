# Information architecture — bitibybit.com homepage

Written against `docs/review/01-evidence.md` (facts) and `docs/review/02-diagnosis.md`
(settled positioning). Repo at `0cec1dd`, 31 August 2026. Neither is re-derived
here.

Three rules held throughout:

- **The homepage is a discovery interface.** For every element: does it help
  someone find the right tool? Explanation goes to the tool page or goes.
- **Nothing claims observed behaviour.** `.stats-history.jsonl` still has one
  row. Where I reason from heuristic it says so and names the measurement.
- **Every recommendation names a file and survives the build.** The constraint
  audit is §G at the end; I checked each claim against the script rather than
  assuming.

---

# The IA question, decided before anything is designed

Five models were on the table. Three the brief named, two I tested because they
were the strongest available alternatives.

## Model 1 — Noun categories (what ships today)

*Sweeps & draws · Gifts & occasions · Food & parties · Care & kindness ·
Deciding & planning · Every day.*

These are categories of **tool**, sorted by what the tool is. Two of the six
("Gifts & occasions", "Food & parties") are half occasion-shaped by accident;
the rest are product vocabulary.

**Where it breaks, from the evidence, not from taste:** *Care & kindness* holds
one tool. *Fact Matcher* sits under *Food & parties* while its three guide pages
are `icebreaker-questions/`, `standup-games/` and `board-meeting-icebreakers/`.
*Secret Role Dealer* sits beside a potluck board and a volunteer roster. Three
independent misfilings in six shelves is not bad copywriting; it is a taxonomy
whose axis doesn't fit the objects.

## Model 2 — Job-to-be-done router ("I need to…")

*I need to draw names · split people · collect things from everyone · fill
shifts · get a decision · say thanks.*

**Why it's attractive:** verbs are precise, they're what the tool actually does,
and they collapse the 22 to six mechanisms with no misfilings — every tool has
exactly one mechanism.

**Why it loses:** the mechanism is the thing the visitor doesn't know yet. A
person does not arrive thinking "I need to collect one item from each of thirty
people with per-category capacities." They arrive thinking "the fete is in three
weeks." Naming the mechanism asks them to do the translation the site should be
doing for them. This is the same failure as Model 1 with better words: it is
still product vocabulary, just in the imperative.

The exception is the visitor who *does* know the mechanism — someone who wants
planning poker, a bracket, a random team split. That's a real population, and
Model 2 is right for them. It is right for a minority, and it is a shelf at the
bottom of the page, not the page's organising idea. It survives in §E as
section 5.

## Model 3 — Occasion model ("Organise a…")

*A farewell · a wedding · the school fete · someone's unwell · Christmas · the
office sweep.*

**Why it wins:** it is the only one of the three in which the labels are things
a person says out loud about their own week without having seen the site. It is
also the model the site already believes in and already runs — the seasonal
feature card is an occasion router with a sample size of one, chosen by the
clock. `SEASONS` at `src/worker.js:179-195` is five occasions tiling a year.
The architecture exists; it is applied to 1 element out of 30.

The second piece of internal evidence is the one the diagnosis found:
`public/llms.txt` describes Meal Train as "a roster of who's cooking which
night for a new parent, someone unwell, or a family having a hard week." That
is occasion writing, and it is addressed to machines. The site knows how to do
this and stops doing it for humans.

**Where it genuinely breaks, and this matters:** occasions are **many-to-many**
and headings are **one-to-many**. Group Card belongs to a farewell *and* a 40th
*and* a new baby *and* a retirement. Bring a Plate belongs to Christmas *and*
the street party *and* the fete. A heading forces one filing and throws the rest
away — which is structurally the same crime as Model 1, committed with nicer
words. Any design that solves the taxonomy by renaming the six headings to
occasions has moved the bug, not fixed it.

And a hard fact: **five of the 22 have no occasion at all.** Team Picker,
Tournament Bracket, Group Vote, Scrum Poker and Secret Role Dealer are reached
for by name, by people who already know what they want. Forcing an occasion
heading onto them is dishonest and would produce a fresh set of misfilings.

## Model 4 (tested) — No categories. One flat, ordered shelf of 22

**Why I tested it:** 22 items sits right at the edge where grouping stops
paying. Six headings across 22 cards is 3.7 cards per shelf — the heading costs
nearly as much attention as the card it introduces.

**What it costs, measured rather than asserted.** From the evidence's own
heading positions at 390px: between *Care & kindness* (2005px, one tool) and
*Deciding & planning* (2177px) is 172px = one card row + one heading. Between
*Deciding & planning* (2177px, two tools) and *Every day* (2349px) is also
172px = one card row + one heading. Between *Sweeps & draws* (684px, five
tools) and *Gifts & occasions* (1130px) is 446px = three card rows + one
heading. Solving: **one card row ≈ 137px, one heading ≈ 35px.** Six headings
therefore cost about **210px** at mobile, roughly a quarter of a viewport.
(Consistent with `public/styles.css:411`, which scopes `#tools h2` to `.74rem`
with `1.5rem/.6rem` margins under 34rem, once margin collapse against the grid
is allowed for.)

**Why I rejected it:** 210px is real but it is not decisive, and I want to be
explicit about that, because "headings are expensive" would be a convenient
argument and it is a weak one. The reason to change the headings is that they
are *wrong*, not that they cost 210px. Removing them entirely costs the
landmarks a returning visitor navigates by, and — more importantly — it removes
the anchor targets that any router has to point at without shipping JavaScript
(see Model 5).

## Model 5 (tested) — Occasion filter chips over a flat shelf

Tap "Christmas", the grid reorders or filters to the six matching cards. This is
the design that actually solves many-to-many: one card reachable from three
chips.

**Why I rejected it, and this is the constraint that decided the whole
architecture.** A filter is a control, and a control needs JavaScript. The chips
would either be in the static HTML and inert until JS loads, or injected by JS.
Injecting a ~90px element above the fold on load is precisely the mistake this
codebase has already paid for once — `scripts/gen-live-preview.mjs` exists
because rendering the preview on load cost 12% of CLS samples rated poor, with
Cloudflare naming the element (`scripts/check-qotd-preview.mjs` is the
write-up). Filtering also *hides tools*, and the tools it hides are the ones
nobody knows exist, which is the opposite of discovery.

I also tested a middle version — chips that reorder rather than hide — and it
fails the same CLS test and adds a second behaviour to a control that already
has one.

## The choice

**Occasion model, in two layers, plus one honest mechanism shelf.**

The insight that resolves the many-to-many problem is that this site already
has a many-to-many layer and it is not navigation — **it is the card blurb.**
Group Card's long blurb reads "Farewells, new babies, big birthdays,
retirements." Four occasions, one card, no taxonomy, no control to learn, works
with no JavaScript, and it is *read* rather than *operated*. Bring a Plate's
"Stops six pavlovas and no salad" is a complete situation in 32 characters.

So:

| Layer | Cardinality | Mechanism | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Occasion → tool** | many-to-many | card blurbs naming the occasions | `.tool-desc` (uncapped, desktop) and `.tool-desc-short` (44-char cap, mobile) |
| **Tool → shelf** | one-to-many | five section headings, occasion-shaped where honest | `#tools h2` |
| **Occasion → entry point** | many-to-many | six plain links to card anchors | a new row above the shelf |
| **Season → one bet** | one-to-one, per request | the existing `SEASONS` clock | `src/worker.js:179-195` |

The current site's failure, stated precisely: **its headings are mechanism
nouns while its blurbs are situations.** The two layers disagree, and the
heading — being larger, higher and read first — wins. Fixing the headings is
the whole job. The blurbs were already right.

## How I would tell if I'm wrong

In the order I'd actually run them, cheapest and most decisive first.

1. **Is `/` even the router?** Cloudflare Web Analytics → Pages. Compare entries
   to `/` against direct entries to the 22 tool root pages and the 26 guide
   pages. The guide pages are the SEO surface by design; `sitemap.xml` lists 52
   URLs and `/` is one of them. **If most sessions land on a tool or guide page
   from search and never touch `/`, this entire document is a low-stakes
   refactor** and the attention belongs on guide-page → tool-page conversion
   instead. Run this before building anything. It costs one dashboard visit.
2. **The metric, if `/` matters:** click-through from `/` into any of the 22
   tool root pages. Baseline it for two weeks *before* changing the page. Not
   "time on page", not "scroll depth" — this page's only job is to send people
   somewhere else, and the only good outcome is a departure.
3. **The falsifier for the occasion model specifically.** The six occasion links
   point at six specific cards. If those six tools' `made` counts in
   `scripts/stats.mjs` do not move relative to the sixteen they don't point at,
   the occasion hypothesis is wrong and the vocabulary problem is upstream at
   Google, not on this page. This requires §A.4 of the diagnosis to have
   happened first: one row in `.stats-history.jsonl` cannot falsify anything.
   **Write the second row before touching the HTML.**
4. **The test that needs no code and no data.** Five people who have organised a
   group thing. Give each a situation out loud — "your kid's school fete is in
   three weeks and you need parents on the barbecue" — and time them to the
   first correct card, on a phone. Current page against a paper mock. n=5 is not
   statistics and I won't pretend otherwise, but it will surface a wrong heading
   in ten minutes, and it is the only instrument available that measures
   *finding* rather than *arriving*.
5. **The signal that Model 4 was right after all:** if in testing people scroll
   straight past the headings to scan card names, the headings are decoration
   and should go. Watch where the thumb stops.

# C. Homepage information architecture, top to bottom

Eight bands. Two are unchanged, one is conditional, one is deleted.

---

## 1. Site header

**Purpose.** Identity, and a way back.

**Content.** Unchanged: wordmark, `beta` badge, `tools`, `about`.

**UI.** Unchanged.

**Why it belongs here.** It doesn't do discovery work; it does orientation
work, and it's 40px.

**One change.** `<a href="#tools">tools</a>` currently lands on the first
category heading. Point it at the occasion row instead (band 4). Tapping "tools"
should land on the router, not on the first shelf.

**What it displaces.** Nothing.

---

## 2. Hero — headline and three-beat lede

**Purpose.** Answer *what is this, why is it different from a spreadsheet, and
what happens to the people I send it to* — in one screen, before any choosing
starts.

**Content.**

- `<h1>` — **"Small free tools for groups"**, unchanged. Settled in §B.2 of the
  diagnosis: it names the shelf accurately in five words a stranger can repeat
  back. Swapping it trades a plain label for a slogan.
- Lede, resequenced into the three beats the diagnosis settled, strongest first:

  1. **The problem, in the reader's words.** "The little organisational jobs
     that usually end in a spreadsheet and three reminder messages."
  2. **The mechanism.** "Make a thing, share a link, done."
  3. **The participant promise** — which does not exist anywhere in the hero
     today. "Nobody signs up, installs anything, or gets added to anything.
     Not you, and not them."

**UI.** Unchanged markup: `<section class="hero">`, `h1`, `p.lede`. Beat 3 adds
roughly one line, ~28px at mobile.

**Why it belongs here.** Beat 3 is the only band on the page carrying the site's
actual differentiator. Per the evidence, the strongest, most defensible,
hardest-to-copy fact about this product — a participant is two taps and zero
typing from done, installs nothing, is emailed nothing — currently lives in a
privacy page and a code comment. It belongs above the fold or it belongs
nowhere.

**What it displaces.** ~28px, and the word order of the existing lede. Beat 3
should also replace the `og:description` string on this page, currently "Small
free tools for getting a group of people to do something" — that string is what
renders when someone pastes the *homepage* into a group chat, which is by
definition a group of people about to be organised.

---

## 3. "Things you've made in this browser" — conditional strip

**Purpose.** Retrieval. Get a returning organiser back to the thing they made,
and remind them the site exists between occasions.

**Content.** Up to five entries from the shared `bbb:made:v1` key proposed in
diagnosis §A.5 (`{tool, title, editUrl, at}`), each a link to its `/e/` URL.
One line of fine print: these live in this browser and nowhere else.

**UI.** A quiet single-column strip of text links — not cards, not a grid.
Hidden entirely when the key is empty, which is the state for every first-time
visitor. Give it `class="prev-sweeps"`, which is **already in `SKIP_CLASSES`**
in `scripts/gen-markdown.mjs:33-35` — per-browser state should not be frozen
into a static `.md` twin, and reusing the existing class gets that for free.

**Why it belongs here, this high.** Because it is *retrieval*, and retrieval
failure is worse than discovery failure. Losing your `/e/` link means the thing
you made is unreachable; missing the seasonal card means you scroll. For the
population it is non-empty for, it is the highest-value element on the page, and
it costs everyone else exactly zero pixels.

**What it displaces.** Nothing for a first-time visitor. For a returning maker,
~50px of the occasion row's position — which is the correct trade, since a
returning maker needs the router less than a stranger does.

**Note.** This band assumes diagnosis §A.5. If that isn't built, the band
doesn't exist and everything below moves up. Nothing else here depends on it.

---

## 4. The occasion row — the router

**Purpose.** Put situation vocabulary into the first viewport, where the
evidence says there is currently none, and give a person who knows their
occasion but not the mechanism a one-tap path to the right neighbourhood.

**Content.** A short label and six links. Exact copy in §E.2.

> **I'm organising…**
> Someone's leaving · The school fete · A new baby · Someone's unwell ·
> A wedding · Christmas

**UI.** `<ul>` of plain anchor links, wrapping to two or three lines at 390px,
styled as small pills. About 90px at mobile.

**Two markup rules that are not cosmetic:**

- **In the static HTML, not injected.** No JavaScript. No layout shift on load.
  See Model 5 above and `check-qotd-preview.mjs` for why this is not negotiable.
- **Not wrapped in `<nav>`.** `nav` is in `SKIP_TAGS` at
  `scripts/gen-markdown.mjs:27-29`, so a `<nav>` wrapper deletes the row from
  the page's `.md` twin. This row is the richest occasion vocabulary on the page
  and it should reach the Markdown surface. A `<ul>` inside the `#tools` section
  is also the more accurate semantics: it's a table of contents for the shelf
  below it, not site navigation.

**Why it belongs here — above the feature card, not below.** The row is right
for every visitor and costs ~90px. The feature card is right for one seasonal
segment and costs ~200px. The cheap thing that is always right goes first. A
visitor who wants the seasonal card has not lost it — it is enormous and
directly below.

**What it displaces, stated plainly.** ~90px, which pushes the feature card, the
first shelf heading and the first two tool cards below the fold at 390×812.
**The first viewport will stop showing any tool cards.** I think that is the
right trade and I'll defend it: 2 of 22 cards in the first screen was never
discovery — it was an accident of where the fold happened to fall, and which two
cards you saw was determined by nothing in particular. Six occasion words that
name the reader's own week are worth more than two arbitrary cards.

**Design cost worth naming.** Placing a row of small text above the page's
largest visual weakens the hook. That is a real cost and it is a visual design
problem, not an IA one — it is solvable with type and spacing, and I would not
solve it by moving the row.

---

## 5. The seasonal feature card

**Purpose.** One editorial bet, placed by the clock, on the tool that is right
for the largest number of visitors *this week*.

**Content and mechanism.** Unchanged. `SEASONS` at `src/worker.js:179-195`,
rendered per request from the Sydney-local date via `HTMLRewriter`, with
`scripts/check-seasons.mjs` walking all 366 days of a leap year to prove the
five windows tile the year with no gap and no overlap. The static September
markup underneath stays as the no-JS fallback.

**UI.** Unchanged.

**Why it belongs here.** It is the only element on the page that can be right
about *time*, and time is half of an occasion. It also does the job that a
hand-picked "flagship" tries to do and fails at: an editor's bet goes stale the
moment deploys stop; a clock's bet is wrong for the same duration but can never
go stale. Its own comment says this.

**What it displaces.** ~90px of position, and one deletion:
**`public/index.html:309-353` — the client-side `picks` array — goes.** This is
diagnosis §A.3, and it is a precondition for keeping this band at all. Two
independent seasonal implementations disagree on 234 of 365 days; the client one
overwrites the server one and is validated by nothing in `scripts/`. From 1 Jan
2027 the largest element on the homepage would read "Footy finals · September"
for 226 consecutive days. A card that is wrong for eight months is not an
editorial slot, it is a liability, and I would not give it 200px above the shelf
until that script is deleted.

**What I considered and rejected.** Featuring a second tool. Two features is a
shelf, and the page already has one further down that is better at being a
shelf.

---

## 6. The shelf — five sections, all 22 tools

**Purpose.** The completeness guarantee. Everything the site can do, visible, in
one scan, with no control to operate and nothing hidden behind a tap.

**Content.** Five occasion-shaped sections and 22 cards. Full specification in
§E.1.

**UI.** Unchanged card and grid CSS. Three additions:

- An `id` on each `<a class="tool-card">`, so the occasion row has something to
  point at. 22 attributes, zero pixels.
- `scroll-margin-top` on `.tool-card` (~4rem) so an anchored arrival leaves the
  section heading visible above the card, and a `:target` rule so the arrived-at
  card is marked. Four lines of CSS, no JavaScript.
- One count line under the first heading: **"22 tools. All free, none need an
  account."** The evidence records that the first viewport gives no indication
  of how many tools exist. A count is the cheapest possible reason to keep
  scrolling.

  > **Superseded, 31 Aug 2026 — the line moved ABOVE the first heading.**
  > "Under the first heading" was right for the h2 this was written against: an
  > 11.84px uppercase eyebrow in `--ink-faint`, which owned nothing beneath it,
  > so the line read as an introduction to the whole shelf. `07-visual.md` V2
  > promoted h2 to a 1.3rem sentence-case heading in `--ink`, and a heading that
  > size owns what follows it — so "22 tools" was captured by "A farewell, a
  > wedding, a new baby" and read as a claim about a section that holds six.
  > The sentence did not move; the thing above it grew. It is now a
  > `.shelf-note` immediately above the first `<h2>`, which costs nothing: its
  > two margins add up to exactly the 1.6rem the heading's top margin was
  > already spending, so the first tool card still clears the fold at 689px.

**Why it belongs here.** Because a router without a shelf beneath it is a
guessing game. **The shelf is what makes the router safe to be incomplete** —
six occasion links do not have to cover all 22 situations, because everything
they miss is 400px below in plain sight. This is what dissolves the orphan
problem the diagnosis worried about with Secret Role Dealer: an occasion router
that must account for every tool becomes a taxonomy again.

**What it displaces.**

- Six mechanism headings become five occasion headings. Net cost about **+40px**
  at mobile (five headings that wrap to two lines at ~50px each, against six
  one-liners at ~35px). I'll take a 40px penalty for headings that are true.
- The one-item *Care & kindness* shelf ceases to exist; Meal Train joins Bring a
  Plate and Volunteer Roster, which is one situation that was split across three
  shelves.
- Fact Matcher and Secret Role Dealer leave *Food & parties*, which is the
  misfiling the evidence recorded.
- Nothing is removed from the page. No tool is hidden, no card is truncated, no
  "show more" is added. See §E.4.

---

## 7. Guides & how-tos

**Purpose.** The SEO surface, linked from the homepage.

**Content and UI.** Unchanged. All 24 links, same flat list, same section note.

**Why it belongs here — and why I am explicitly not touching it.** A homepage
that is being tightened for discovery is exactly the page where 24 rows of small
link text look like clutter, and cutting them would be the obvious tidying move.
Don't. `sitemap.xml` lists 52 URLs; `robots.txt` disallows `/s/`, `/e/`, `/p/`,
`/api/` and `/mcp` for 30+ named agents, and `src/lib.js:404` sets `noindex` on
every `pageShell()` render. **Only home, the 22 tool root pages and the 26 guide
pages carry any search weight at all**, which makes 24 homepage links into the
guide set a load-bearing internal-link signal, not decoration.

It also does discovery work by accident: it is currently the *only* place on the
homepage where the words "school fete", "canteen", "sausage sizzle", "book club"
and "board meeting" appear. The occasion row (band 4) lifts that vocabulary to
the top of the page where it can be found; the guide list keeps it here where it
can be crawled.

**What it displaces.** Nothing. This is a deliberate non-change.

---

## 8. Footer / about

**Purpose.** Provenance, privacy posture, and the links a small number of people
specifically want.

**Content.** Unchanged except one trim. "What is this? Little tools for
organising a group of people — free, no accounts, no fuss. You make a thing, you
share a link, everyone's sorted. Made in Australia, with new tools added often."
After the lede rewrite in band 2, the first two sentences are a restatement of
beats 1 and 2 a page-length later. Cut to the provenance and the mechanism:
*"Made in Australia. You make a thing, you share a link, everyone's sorted. New
tools added often."*

**UI.** Unchanged. Note it carries `class="site-foot"`, which is in
`SKIP_CLASSES` — the footer is already absent from the `.md` twin and should
stay that way.

**Why it belongs here.** Nobody discovers a tool in a footer. It is the right
place for the coffee link, the source link, the privacy link and the API docs,
and none of those compete for attention up the page.

**What it displaces.** ~20px and one redundancy.

# E. Tool discovery

## E.1 The five sections, in order, with all 22 tools

Ordering principle for the sections: **how often the situation occurs**, not how
big it is when it does. Christmas is the largest occasion and it happens once;
someone leaves a workplace constantly. The two hard-dated peaks are already
handled above the shelf by the seasonal card for 5½ months of the year, which
frees the shelf to be ordered by frequency rather than by calendar.

---

### 1 · A farewell, a wedding, a new baby — 6 tools

| # | Tool | id | Moved from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Group Card | `#group-card` | Gifts & occasions |
| 2 | Gift Idea Board | `#gift-ideas` | Gifts & occasions |
| 3 | Baby Guess Pool | `#baby-guess-pool` | **Sweeps & draws** |
| 4 | Pixel Gift Registry | `#gift-registry` | Gifts & occasions |
| 5 | Hens & Shower Planner | `#hens-planner` | **Food & parties** |
| 6 | Recipe Collection | `#recipe-collection` | Gifts & occasions |

Three occasion nouns, not a sentence — at `.74rem` with `.1em` tracking a
heading gets ~25 characters a line at 390px, and "A farewell, a wedding, a new
baby" (32) wraps to two. A sentence-form heading would wrap to three.

Baby Guess Pool leaves *Sweeps & draws*, which the evidence flagged: a
guess-the-date pool is individual prediction against an unknown future outcome,
not random assignment of a fixed set of outcomes. It was grouped by "everyone
puts something in and one person wins", which is a mechanism family, not a
situation. Under this cut it sits beside the other things you do when a baby is
coming, which is how it is actually reached.

**First and largest because** it is the most frequent situation in the catalogue
and the least seasonal — it is true in March.

---

### 2 · Feeding or staffing a crowd — 3 tools

| # | Tool | id | Moved from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bring a Plate | `#bring-a-plate` | Food & parties |
| 2 | Volunteer Roster | `#volunteer-roster` | Food & parties |
| 3 | Meal Train | `#meal-train` | **Care & kindness (sole occupant)** |

This dissolves the one-item shelf. Meal Train, Volunteer Roster and Bring a
Plate are one situation — a group rallying round something — that was split
across three headings.

**The obvious objection:** does Meal Train lose its care framing next to a
potluck board? No, and this is the two-layer design working as designed. The
blurb carries it — "Meals by date when a family needs them" (39 chars, under the
44 cap, unchanged) — and the occasion row carries it — "Someone's unwell" points
directly at this card. The heading is the coarse layer and it does not have to
say everything.

**Second because** the school-and-club window is 15 Feb – 14 Aug, six months of
the year in which the seasonal card is pointing at Volunteer Roster and this
section is where a visitor lands next.

---

### 3 · Christmas and the office sweep — 3 tools

| # | Tool | id | Moved from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kris Kringle | `#kris-kringle` | Gifts & occasions |
| 2 | Grand Final Sweep | `#grand-final-sweep` | Sweeps & draws |
| 3 | Melbourne Cup Sweep | `#melbourne-cup-sweep` | Sweeps & draws |

**Why these three share a shelf, and why it isn't a category error.** Christmas
is one tool; the office sweep is two. Separately they would be a one-item shelf
and a two-item shelf — exactly the *Care & kindness* failure I just spent a
section criticising. The rule that resolves it:

> **An occasion with a hard date and fewer than three tools is served by the
> seasonal card and the occasion row, not by a heading.**

Between 15 Aug and 24 Dec — 132 days — the seasonal card *is* this section,
sized at 200px and placed above everything. This shelf is the off-season path
and the tie-back for the person who scrolled. Labelling it honestly as the two
dated peaks is better than pretending they're one thing or splitting them into
two thin shelves.

**Third because** for 233 days of the year the card above has already moved on,
and for the other 132 the card has already answered.

### 4 · Every Monday — 5 tools

| # | Tool | id | Moved from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kudos Wall | `#kudos-wall` | Every day |
| 2 | Weekly Pulse | `#weekly-pulse` | Every day |
| 3 | Question of the Day | `#question-of-the-day` | Every day |
| 4 | Coffee Roulette | `#coffee-roulette` | Every day |
| 5 | Fact Matcher | `#fact-matcher` | **Food & parties** |

"Every day" becomes "Every Monday" — one word shorter and it names a moment
rather than a frequency. These are the standing tools a manager sets up once.

## E.2 The occasion row — exact copy

```
I'm organising…
  Someone's leaving   →  #group-card          (section 1)
  The school fete     →  #volunteer-roster    (section 2)
  A new baby          →  #baby-guess-pool     (section 1)
  Someone's unwell    →  #meal-train          (section 2)
  A wedding           →  #gift-registry       (section 1)
  Christmas           →  #kris-kringle        (section 3)
```

**Why links to a card, not to a section.** This is what makes the row
many-to-many while the shelf stays one-to-many. Six different occasions land on
six different cards across three sections; "The school fete" and "Someone's
unwell" are different situations that both live in section 2, and pointing them
both at the heading would land both on Bring a Plate. Pointing at the card gets
the exact answer, and `scroll-margin-top` keeps the section heading visible above
it so the neighbours are found too.

**Why the label is "I'm organising…" and not "I need to…".** What follows is a
noun — an occasion — not a verb. "I need to… someone's leaving" doesn't parse.
The brief's job-to-be-done framing survives as the *prompt*; the occasions are
the *options*. That hybrid is deliberate: the verb frame is right for asking the
question and wrong for answering it.

**Why sections 4 and 5 get no pill.** Their headings already say what they are
in the words a person would use ("Every Monday", "Teams, brackets, votes and
games"). A pill duplicating a heading is a control that leads to its own label.
The row exists to close the gap between how a person describes their week and
how the shelf is labelled; where there is no gap, there is no pill.

**Why six and not ten.** Six wraps to two or three lines at 390px (~90px). Ten
would be four lines and would start to read as a tag cloud, which is a thing
people learn to skip. The shelf is 400px below and holds everything the row
misses — the row does not need to be complete, and trying to make it complete
turns it back into a taxonomy.

**Order.** By how often the situation occurs, same principle as the sections.
Christmas is last, which reads oddly in November — and in November the seasonal
card above is already showing Kris Kringle at 200px, so the pill is the
off-season path and frequency ordering is correct.

## E.4 The four decisions, defended

### How many tools show before "more"?

**All 22. There is no "more" control, and no "coming soon" card.**

Four reasons, in order of weight:

1. **The tail is the point.** A truncation control hides the cards nobody knows
   exist, which is precisely the population that needs showing. Nobody taps
   "show 12 more" to discover a thing they have no name for. The tools most
   likely to be hidden by a cutoff — Coffee Roulette, Kudos Wall, Fact Matcher —
   are the ones with the least existing demand.
2. **Length is not the problem; order was.** The whole shelf is ~11 card rows,
   about 1,500px at 390px. That is under two thumb-flicks. The evidence's
   2.9-screens-to-the-last-heading figure is measured from the *top of the
   document*, including a 684px hero and feature card — the shelf itself was
   never the long part.
3. **It breaks the static contract.** Hidden cards are removed from the initial
   paint, from find-in-page, and — depending on implementation — from the `.md`
   twin that `scripts/gen-markdown.mjs` generates for every public page. The
   entire homepage currently works with JavaScript disabled. A "more" button is
   the first thing that wouldn't.
4. **It makes the catalogue's size unknowable.** The evidence records that the
   first viewport gives no indication that 22 tools exist. A cutoff makes that
   permanently true. The count line in band 6 does the opposite job for 20px.

The unused `.tool-card.soon` / `.soon-chip` styles at `public/styles.css:414-421`
should stay unused on this page. A dashed placeholder on a discovery shelf is an
advertisement for something the visitor cannot have.

### Do categories help or hurt?

**As mechanism nouns they hurt. As occasions, and only as a second layer under
the blurb, they help.**

The current headings are a *low-information, sometimes-wrong* layer sitting on
top of a *high-information, always-right* layer. The blurb "Stops six pavlovas
and no salad" tells you more about when to use Bring a Plate than any heading
can. The heading "Food & parties" above Fact Matcher tells you something false.
When they disagree, the heading wins, because it is larger, higher and read
first.

Headings earn their 210px by doing the one thing a blurb cannot: giving a scan
somewhere to stop. Five is the right number for 22 cards — enough to break the
grid into recognisable neighbourhoods, few enough that reading all five headings
is faster than reading all 22 names.

### Is any tool featured?

**Exactly one, and it is chosen by the clock, not by a person.**

The existing seasonal card, unchanged in mechanism. A hand-picked flagship is a
permanent bet that goes stale — the client-side script being deleted in band 5
made exactly that bet, promoting Pixel Gift Registry as "The flagship" for the
nine months between 27 December and 1 September. A clock's bet is wrong for the
same stretch but can never be *stale*, and `check-seasons.mjs` walks all 366 days
to prove the windows tile the year.

No second feature. No "popular" or "most used" badge — with one row in
`.stats-history.jsonl` there is no honest basis for either, and a fabricated
popularity signal is worse than none.

**One thing I considered and rejected:** making the *shelf order* clock-driven,
so the current season's section floats to position 1. It reuses `SEASONS`, and
it's tempting. Rejected for three reasons: it puts a second clock-driven element
on the page (one is a feature, two is a system nobody can reason about); it
changes the page's shape unpredictably for returning visitors, who navigate by
where things were; and moving whole sections through `HTMLRewriter` is a
materially larger change than swapping five text nodes in one card. **One clock
on the page.**

### Do cards need visual previews?

**No. The 56px pixel icons stay and no thumbnails are added.**

1. **A preview answers the wrong question at this moment.** The card's job is
   "is this the thing for my situation" — answered by words. The preview question
   is "what do I actually get" — a commitment question, asked one click later.
2. **That question is already answered better, elsewhere.** `class="see-example"`
   → `/s/demo-*` ships on 21 of 22 tool pages, in the hero, showing a real
   finished thing. That is a live example, not a thumbnail, and it sits at the
   moment of commitment. **This is exactly the brief's rule working: the
   explanation moves to the tool page.**
3. **Thumbnails would not discriminate.** A Bring a Plate board and a Volunteer
   Roster render as near-identical grids of names at 150px. Their blurbs are
   completely distinguishable. The picture is the less informative artefact.
4. **They go stale silently.** 22 screenshots is 22 things that drift from the UI
   with no build check catching it, on a codebase whose defining habit is that
   every derived artefact has a script that fails the build when it diverges.
5. **Weight and CLS.** 22 real renders near the fold on a static page, on a site
   that already has a written incident report about a load-time layout shift.

**The exception proves the rule.** Pixel Gift Registry is the one tool whose
entire value proposition is visual — a picture that fills in as guests claim
parts. Its icon is `/icons/car.png`: a pixel car. The icon system is already
carrying the preview for the one card that needs one.

# F. Search

## The decision: no search box. Not now, and not at 22 tools.

I want to be clear this is not a reflex against a conventional element. I looked
for the strongest case for search on this site, found it, and it still loses.

**The strongest case, stated fairly.** The site has a real vocabulary gap. People
say *secret santa*, not Kris Kringle. *Potluck*, not Bring a Plate. *Sign-up
sheet*, not Volunteer Roster. *Planning poker*, not Scrum Poker. *Wedding
registry*, not Pixel Gift Registry. That is five of 22 — **23% of the catalogue
is named something a visitor will not type.** A vocabulary gap is the textbook
argument for search, and it is genuinely present here.

## Four reasons it still loses

**1. The population with the vocabulary gap is upstream, at Google, not here.**
The person who types "secret santa generator" does it into a search engine, and
`/kris-kringle/rules/` is in `sitemap.xml` and built to catch them. The 26 guide
pages plus 22 tool root pages *are* the search interface for this product, and
they are already the SEO surface by explicit design — `robots.txt` disallows
`/s/`, `/e/` and `/p/` for 30+ named agents and `src/lib.js:404` sets `noindex`
on every `pageShell()` render, so search weight is concentrated on exactly the 49
pages that answer vocabulary questions. **An on-site search box serves only the
population that has already found the site** — the ones already looking at a
shelf of everything the site does.

**2. The real queries here are situations, not keywords, and a 22-item index
cannot answer a situation.** See query 5 below. This is the decisive one.

**3. A zero-result state is worse than a shelf.** Someone types "advent calendar"
or "rsvp" or "split the bill" and gets nothing. A shelf shows them 22 things and
a decent chance of an adjacent answer. Search converts *"not quite that, but
here's something"* into *"this site doesn't have it"*, and they leave. At 22
items the shelf is the more forgiving interface, and the site has no
adjacent-match machinery.

**4. It creates a third source of truth for the card copy.** Today there are two
and they are checked against each other: `public/index.html` and the `SHORT` map
in `scripts/sync-card-copy.mjs`, which throws if a card has no entry *and* if an
entry has no card. A search index is a third list of 22 names plus synonyms, with
nothing enforcing agreement. On a codebase whose defining habit is that every
derived artefact has a script that fails the build on divergence, adding an
unchecked one is out of character for a reason.

There is also a smaller point worth stating: a search field in the hero displaces
the lede, and the lede is where the participant promise is finally getting a slot
(band 2). A form field is the loudest possible element and it would take the
first viewport from a positioning statement to a text input on a site with 22
items.

## What does the job instead

Four things, in order of how much of the gap they close.

### 1. Put every `llms.txt` synonym into the human-visible long blurb

This is the direct substitute and it is better than search on three axes: it
works for people who scan, it works for browser find-in-page, and it works in
Google. The long blurb (`.tool-desc`) has no character cap — only the 44-char
`.tool-desc-short` is budgeted, and it stays untouched.

The gaps, audited against `public/llms.txt`:

| Tool | Synonym the site knows | In `llms.txt` | In the card blurb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kris Kringle | "Secret Santa" | yes | **no** |
| Scrum Poker | "planning poker" | yes | **no** |
| Volunteer Roster | "sign-up sheet", "working bee" | yes | **no** |
| Pixel Gift Registry | "wedding registry", "wishing well" | yes | **no** |
| Coffee Roulette | "random coffee" | **no** | **no** |
| Bring a Plate | "potluck" | yes | yes ✓ |
| Group Vote | "poll" | yes | yes ✓ |
| Fact Matcher | "two truths", "icebreaker" | yes | partly ✓ |

**Five blurbs to edit.** This is the same pattern the diagnosis found with Meal
Train: the site writes its best, most findable copy for machines and stops doing
it for humans. Fixing it is five long-blurb edits, free against the build
(`sync-card-copy.mjs` preserves `longText` verbatim — see §G).

### 2. The occasion row

"Christmas" is the word a person has when they mean Kris Kringle. A tappable
occasion is a synonym router that works by recognition rather than recall, which
is the strictly easier cognitive task and the reason menus beat command lines for
infrequent users. Six pills cover the six most common situations without anyone
typing.

### 3. The shelf itself

22 cards visible in about two thumb-flicks, with no control to operate. At this
catalogue size, scanning *is* the search, and it has a 100% recall rate.

### 4. The guide list

24 links carrying "school fete", "canteen roster", "board meeting", "book club",
"sausage sizzle", "would you rather". Crawlable, and findable with find-in-page.

## The five queries, run against the design above

The brief asks for five example queries and the result each should return. Since
I'm declining the search box, here is the same test used as a falsifier: for each
query, what a person actually types or thinks, and whether this design gets them
there. If it fails these, I'm wrong.

| # | Query | Should return | Does this design get them there? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"secret santa"** | Kris Kringle | **Yes, two ways.** Google → `/kris-kringle/rules/`, already in the sitemap and written for this query. On-site → the "Christmas" pill lands on `#kris-kringle`. **Fix required:** "Secret Santa" must go into the long blurb, or find-in-page fails. |
| 2 | **"potluck"** | Bring a Plate | **Yes, already.** The long blurb reads "The potluck board that prevents six pavlovas and no salad." Find-in-page finds it; the section heading "Feeding or staffing a crowd" sits directly above. No change needed. |
| 3 | **"school fete roster"** | Volunteer Roster | **Yes, three ways.** The "The school fete" pill → `#volunteer-roster`; the guide list carries `/volunteer-roster/school-fete-roster/` verbatim; and Google has a dedicated page for it. This query is better served by the current site than any search box could serve it. |
| 4 | **"planning poker"** | Scrum Poker | **Yes, weakly.** Section 5's heading is "Teams, brackets, votes and games" and the card is named Scrum Poker — near-synonyms in the same jargon family, so recognition is likely. **Fix required:** "planning poker" into the long blurb. This is the query where a search box would have helped most, and the fix is one sentence. |
| 5 | **"something for a family whose mum just had surgery"** | Meal Train | **Yes — and this is the query that decides §F.** There is no keyword in that sentence matching "Meal Train". A name-and-synonym index returns **zero results** and the person leaves. The "Someone's unwell" pill returns exactly the right card in one tap. To make search answer query 5 you would need a semantic index — a whole system — for 22 items that fit on a screen and a half. |

Query 5 is the argument. **The queries people actually have on this site are
situations, and a keyword search over 22 names cannot answer a situation.** The
occasion row can, because a human wrote the mapping once.

## What would change my mind

Three specific triggers. Any one of them, and search becomes correct.

1. **The catalogue passes ~35 tools**, or the shelf stops fitting in three
   thumb-flicks at 390px. Scanning has a recall cliff and 22 is comfortably under
   it; 35+ is not.
2. **The blurb-synonym fix ships and query 1 and 4 still fail** a five-person
   test. That would mean the vocabulary gap is not closable in copy, which is the
   premise this recommendation rests on.
3. **Cloudflare Web Analytics shows `/` is a major entry point *and* its
   click-through into tool pages is poor after the IA change.** That would mean
   people are arriving at the shelf and failing to choose, which is a findability
   failure that a router did not fix.

If search is ever built, one note on how: **do not put the query in a URL
parameter.** The site's stated position is on `public/index.html:355-361` and it
is about not knowing what people do. A `?q=` log is a query corpus, which is
tempting for exactly the reason it shouldn't exist here.
